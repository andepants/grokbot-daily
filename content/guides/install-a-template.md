A template is a recipe, not a copy. The official guide uses that exact framing: you get the bot’s instructions, relevant memories, skills and plugins, and your bot builds its own version from them.

## What comes with it, and what doesn’t

- **Comes with it:** instructions, the memories the author chose to share, skills, and the list of plugins it uses.
- **Doesn’t:** secrets, personal memories, custom code, scripts, or non-standard plugins like MCP servers. If the bot depends on those, it needs a setup step.

## The five-minute checklist

1. **Read the preview first.** The template page lists its memories, skills, routines and integrations. If a “newsletter bot” wants your email *and* your calendar *and* your files, ask why.
2. **Add it, then check what actually arrived.** Open the new bot and confirm the skills you saw in the preview are there.
3. **Reconnect plugins with your own accounts.** Plugins come over as a list, not as logins.
4. **Keep its routines off.** Run one task yourself, supervised, before turning on any schedule.
5. **Give it your rules.** Drafts instead of sends, nothing posted for you, nothing paid for without asking.

## Updates don’t arrive on their own

When the author improves a template, your copy doesn’t change. To pick up updates you reinstall the template, then reapply your own rules.
